## 2. Ideal customer profile and buyer

> **ASSUMED — not validated.** Everything in this section is a working hypothesis for modeling and must be validated by customer interviews before it drives build or spend decisions.

- **ICP:** 10–100 employee service businesses (home services, logistics/dispatch, agencies, clinics, professional services) where the owner or COO still touches operations daily.
- **Economic buyer:** Owner, COO, or RevOps/operations leader — the person who feels the pain of "too many apps, nobody follows up, everything lives in someone's head."
- **User buyer:** Office manager / dispatcher / ops coordinator — runs the daily workflow inside the product.
- **Why this ICP:** Large enough to pay $100–$400/mo and generate real agent workload; small enough that a 1–3 person vendor team can onboard them; underserved by HubSpot (too expensive/complex) and well-served-but-not-automated by GoHighLevel/Zoho (apps without agents).
- **Anti-ICP:** Solo freelancers (can't pay, churn fast), 500+ employee enterprises (procurement cycles, security questionnaires the MVP can't pass), highly regulated clinical/financial workflows at MVP (governance rail is nascent).

## 3. Pricing model

### 3.1 Pricing dimensions (stored as data, not hardcoded)

The billing system must treat these as configurable dimensions from day one, because the metered dimension is where the margin lives or dies:

```yaml
pricing_dimensions:
  platform_fee:        # base subscription per tier (recurring, per month)
  seats:               # per-seat fee beyond included seats
  agent_entitlements:  # number of active agents / workflows included per tier
  execution_credits:   # included metered units per month
  credit_overage_rate: # price per credit beyond included (per tier)
  model_usage:         # sub-meter: blended into credits; premium models draw more credits per task
  communications:      # SMS/voice/email — PASS-THROUGH at provider cost + small markup, never bundled flat
  storage:             # included GB per tier; overage per GB
  integrations:        # premium connectors (NetSuite, Salesforce, etc.) as add-ons
  support_tier:        # included support level; dedicated CSM as add-on
```

**Credit definition (ASSUMED, must be instrumented):** 1 execution credit = one normalized agent task unit. Model routing assigns credit cost: routine task on a cheap model ≈ 1 credit; reasoning-heavy task on a frontier model ≈ 3–5 credits; each retry/loop iteration draws additional credits. This makes the customer's bill track the vendor's cost curve.

### 3.2 Suggested tiers (concrete, in USD)

| | Starter | Professional | Scale | Enterprise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Platform fee | $49/mo | $149/mo | $399/mo | Custom |
| Included seats | 3 | 10 | 25 | Unlimited/custom |
| Extra seat | $9/mo | $12/mo | $15/mo | Custom |
| Execution credits included | 500/mo | 2,500/mo | 10,000/mo | Custom pool |
| Credit overage | $0.15 | $0.12 | $0.10 | Custom |
| Active agents | 3 | 10 | 30 | Unlimited |
| Human-approval workflows | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Governance rail (OPA policies, audit export) | — | Basic | Full | Full + custom |
| Premium integrations | — | 2 included | 5 included | Unlimited |
| Support | Community + docs | Email, 1 business day | Priority + onboarding call | SLA + CSM |
| Communications | Pass-through | Pass-through | Pass-through | Pass-through |

**Design notes:**
- No free tier at launch (**ASSUMED** decision): a free tier for an agent product is an unbounded-cost attack surface. Offer a 14-day trial with a capped credit pool instead.
- Annual prepay: 2 months free (standard SaaS practice; improves cash and commitment).
- Overage must require an explicit spend cap / budget per customer — see §7 failure modes.
- Communications are **never** bundled into credits: SMS/voice/email ride at provider cost + 10–20% handling (**ASSUMED** markup), itemized separately, exactly as GoHighLevel treats usage/add-ons.

### 3.3 Why this shape (vs. pure seat pricing)

Pure per-seat pricing (HubSpot's model) disconnects price from the vendor's actual cost driver, which is *agent execution*, not logins. Pure usage pricing (Twilio's model) terrifies SMB buyers who want predictable bills. The hybrid — **platform fee + included credits + overages + pass-through comms** — gives the buyer a predictable base and gives the vendor a cost-tracking meter. This is the standard answer to the AI gross-margin problem in §6.

At that blend, 500 included credits cost the vendor roughly **$12.50–$25.00** at 50% utilization — the margin engine is *breakage* (unused credits) plus overage markup, exactly like gym memberships. If utilization runs hot (power users redeeming 100% on frontier models), the same tier goes margin-negative. This is why §7.1 is the single biggest risk.

### 5.4 What the scenarios prove

1. **Inference alone does not kill the business** — at disciplined routing it is 25–35% of revenue, survivable.
2. **Support labor is the silent killer at every scale.** A 10-person service business expects a human to answer the phone. If each customer needs even 2 hours/month of human support at $40/hr loaded, that's $80/mo against $49–$149/mo of revenue. The product must deflect support into the agent layer (dogfooding) or the math never works.
3. **Fixed cloud is the least of your worries** until ~1,000 customers; the modular monolith keeps this cheap early — that architectural choice is economically load-bearing.
4. Break-even on a per-customer basis requires: blended task cost ≤ $0.10, utilization ≤ 60%, and support ≤ 30 min/customer/month by customer #100. All **ASSUMED** targets.

## 6. Onboarding, support cost, and CAC payback

### 6.1 Onboarding (ASSUMED)

- Self-serve onboarding target: < 2 hours of vendor labor per Starter customer (template-based setup).
- Assisted onboarding (Professional/Scale): 4–8 hours incl. integration mapping; charge **$500–$1,500 one-time** implementation fee (positions against HubSpot's mandatory $1,500–$3,500 onboarding — see §8).
- Every onboarding hour is tracked against the customer's 90-day expected margin. If onboarding cost > 3 months of platform fee, the deal needs an implementation fee or a no.

### 6.2 CAC and payback (ASSUMED figures, benchmark-anchored)

- SMB SaaS CAC payback target < 12 months; reported 2026 SMB median 8–12 months; acquisition costs reportedly up 40–60% since 2023 (**LIKELY**, secondary benchmark — https://www.saasmag.com/saas-cac-payback-period-new-growth-gauge-2026/).
- Worked example: CAC $400, Starter ARPU $49/mo at 60% contribution margin → $29.40/mo contribution → **payback ≈ 13.6 months** — misses the 12-month target. Levers: annual prepay (cash upfront), implementation fee (offsets CAC), or land at Professional ($149 → payback ≈ 4.5 months). **Conclusion: the business should aim to land at Professional or attach implementation fees; pure $49/mo self-serve does not pay back acquisition at realistic CAC.**

### 6.3 Acquisition channels (fit for a solo/small team)

1. **Agency white-label** — the strongest fit given the operator's agency: agencies resell the platform to their clients (mirrors GoHighLevel's $497 SaaS Pro motion). Near-zero CAC per end customer; the agency absorbs onboarding/support.
2. **Vertical partnerships** — one integration + co-marketing per vertical (e.g. dispatch software for logistics, booking tools for home services).
3. **GHL/HubSpot migrators** — target businesses already paying for automation tools but getting no agentic execution; comparison content (§8) is the hook.
4. **Template marketplace SEO** — each vertical template is a landing page ("AI dispatcher for trucking companies").
5. **Founder-led content** — build-in-public on the operator's existing distribution (400k+ IG per operator's bio — **user-stated, unverified**).
6. Explicitly **avoid** at this stage: paid search against HubSpot/Zoho keywords (CAC suicide), AppSumo-style lifetime deals (destroys the recurring + overage economics).

## 9. Economic failure modes and mitigations

### 9.1 🔴 THE SINGLE BIGGEST ECONOMIC RISK

> **Unbounded agent execution — retries, tool calls, web searches, code execution, human review labor, communications, onboarding, and SMB support — sold under an inadequately metered flat subscription, producing negative contribution margin even when nominal inference is cheap.**

Token prices are the *visible* cost. The actual margin killers, in order:

1. **Retry/loop storms** — a stuck agent retrying a failing tool 50× at $0.50/task = $25 of cost against a $49/mo subscription. One bad workflow can erase a customer's entire monthly margin in an afternoon.
2. **Human review labor** — a "human-in-the-loop" step that takes 3 minutes of staff time at $30/hr loaded costs $1.50 — 10–100× the inference cost of the task it supervises.
3. **Support labor** (§5.4) — 2 hrs/mo/customer of human support at $40/hr = $80 against $49–$149 revenue.
4. **Communications** — an agent that sends 2,000 SMS/month ($0.015/segment ≈ $30) inside a flat bundle.
5. **Onboarding** — 10 hours of white-glove setup against a $49/mo Starter = 4+ months of revenue burned on day one.

**Mitigations (must be in the MVP, not later):**
- Hard per-customer, per-agent, and per-workflow **budgets and kill-switches**; spending stops, never surprises.
- **Loop/retry caps** in the control plane (max iterations, max cost per task) — architectural, from the agent-control-plane patterns.
- **Model routing**: cheap models by default, frontier only on explicit escalation or policy.
- **Included credits + paid overages** (§3), never unlimited flat.
- **Pass-through communications**, itemized, never bundled.
- **Per-customer cost attribution dashboard** — the vendor must see margin per customer in near-real-time; a customer going margin-negative triggers review before the invoice does.
- Customer-facing **spend caps** default ON; raising the cap is an explicit, logged action.

### 9.2 Other failure modes

| Failure mode | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Provider price hike (e.g. Sonnet intro pricing ended 2026-08-31 — **CONFIRMED**) | Multi-provider routing; credits are provider-agnostic so cost can be rebalanced without repricing |
| One power user redeems 100% of credits on frontier models, then churns | Utilization monitoring; tier usage policies; annual contracts for heavy users |
| Overage bill shock → chargebacks + churn | Default spend caps; progressive alerts at 50/80/100%; no silent overages |
| Support ticket scaling linearly with customers | Dogfood the agent layer for L1 support; template-led onboarding; agency channel absorbs support |
| Lifetime-deal / heavy discounting destroys recurring economics | No LTDs; discounts only via annual prepay |
| Building Enterprise features (SSO, VPC, custom DPA) for $49/mo customers | Hard tier gating; Enterprise tier is custom-priced or it doesn't exist |

## 10. Commercialization roadmap (phases)

- **Phase 0 — Paid beta (now → first 10 customers):** Manual onboarding, founder-led support, generous credit caps with hard kill-switches. Goal: instrument true per-customer cost. Do not scale acquisition until contribution margin per customer is measured, not modeled.
- **Phase 1 — Agency channel (10 → 100):** Launch white-label agency tier; templates for 2–3 verticals; cost-attribution dashboard internal. Goal: CAC ≈ $0 per end customer via agencies.

- **Phase 3 — Upmarket optionality (1,000+):** Enterprise tier (SSO, VPC, audit export, DPA) only when the governance rail is genuinely enterprise-grade. Never before.
